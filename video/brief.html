Dipper

Find urban stream pollution at its source.

The problem

Urban streams are polluted by sewage that should never reach them: household plumbing misconnected to the surface water drain, leaking sewers, and overflows after rain. In the UK alone, an estimated 150,000 to 500,000 homes have a misconnected drain (CIWEM).

Knowing a stream is dirty is easy. Finding which pipe is responsible is hard: a stream has dozens of outfalls, discharges come and go, and crews walk the bank one pipe at a time. The people who notice first report into a void and never hear back. The recast EU Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive (2024/3019) now requires integrated plans covering storm overflows and urban runoff for every agglomeration of 100,000 p.e. and above by 2033.

How it works

  1. Reports become evidence. Each report is snapped to a stream network built from OpenStreetMap, with flow direction, culverts and bridged gaps.
  2. One belief over what and where. An exact joint probability over six explanations and every candidate outfall. Weather (Open Meteo) sets the priors: dry days favour a misconnection, rain an overflow.
  3. Clean results count. A clean look lowers every source upstream of it. Discharges are modelled as intermittent, so a clean check never rules a source out.
  4. The next best check. Every check at every point is scored:
    EVSI + 1.2·bits − cost − 0.1·delay
    Citizen missions are also charged for the walk from where the person reported.
  5. Photos help, people decide. EXIF is stripped and faces are blurred before a vision model (Claude or Gemini) reads a photo, which counts as a weaker observer than any person.

7 stream networks (5 real OneAquaHealth streams, 2 synthetic), 40 trials each, 25-check budget, a simulated world noisier than the model. Dipper doubles the success of a bank walk at lower cost; pure information gain spends about 3.8 times more.

Standards and trust

  • FHIR: nine Dipper profiles on the OneAquaHealth IG (LocationOah, GroupOah), written in FHIR Shorthand, 0 validator errors in CI. Idempotent transaction push.
  • People approve: advisories, handovers and dismissals need a signed-in person with the right role; every decision is in an audit trail.
  • Privacy: anonymous citizens (keyed pseudonyms), private report tokens never in URLs, faces blurred, no IP addresses in logs.
  • Accessibility: 0 axe violations (WCAG 2.2 AA) at desktop, phone and 320 px.

Architecture

One container, deployed on Render. Engine: Python, NumPy, NetworkX. API: FastAPI with roles, hashed tokens, rate limits, a strict CSP and an append only SQLite event store that rebuilds cases by replay. Web: React and MapLibre PWA. CI: 84 tests, accessibility audit, FHIR validation, image build and smoke test.
